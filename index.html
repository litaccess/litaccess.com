What parents should know about LitAccess groups

Who are these groups for?

Groups are designed for K–5 students who would benefit from additional structured literacy instruction and guided practice in a small-group setting.

What happens during a group?

Students receive direct literacy instruction, work on skills at their instructional level, and have guided opportunities to practice what they're learning through reading, discussion, and other activities.

How are students grouped?

Students are grouped by grade, reading skills, and instructional needs so they can learn and practice at an appropriate level with peers who have similar needs.

Is this therapy?

No. LitAccess groups are educational literacy instruction, not speech-language therapy, evaluations, or individualized clinical services. Groups are led by a certified, practicing SLP, but the service is educational rather than clinical.

How often do students meet?

Groups meet once a week for 45 minutes.

What does it cost?

Groups are $199 per month per student and include one 45-minute group session each week, small-group instruction, and LitAccess materials.

What if my child needs a different level?

That's okay. We'll help determine which group is the best fit for your child based on their current reading skills and instructional needs. As your child grows and develops new skills, their group placement may change as needed.

How do I know which group is right for my child?

Request a free group-fit consultation. We'll learn a little about your child's current reading skills and help determine which group may be the best fit.

About LitAccess

Built by an SLP. Designed for education.

LitAccess was developed by a certified, practicing Speech-Language Pathologist who wanted to make high-quality literacy support easier to access and easier to use.

The idea grew from a simple problem: students often need more targeted literacy support, while parents and educators need practical materials and meaningful opportunities for instruction and practice.

LitAccess brings those pieces together through free literacy resources and virtual small-group instruction for K–5 students. The focus is education: useful, focused, accessible support that families and educators can use without hours of preparation.

What LitAccess believes

Literacy support should be accessible, targeted, and useful.

Meet students where they are Instruction and materials should reflect students' current reading skills and support their next steps.
Practice matters Students need repeated, supported opportunities to apply what they are learning.
Keep it practical Resources should be ready to use and useful in real classrooms, homes, and learning settings.
Make support easier to access Families and educators should have practical options for additional literacy support.
Educational literacy groups

Instruction led by an SLP. Designed for education.

LitAccess groups provide educational literacy instruction led by a certified, practicing SLP. They are not speech-language therapy, diagnostic evaluations, or individualized clinical services.

The groups focus on explicit literacy instruction, guided practice, reading, comprehension, vocabulary, fluency, and discussion in a supportive small-group setting. Students work on shared instructional goals with peers at a similar level or with similar skill needs.
